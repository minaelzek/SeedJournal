# Living Sakura v2 — reference analysis

## 1. Identification and classification

- Subject: a stylized mature Japanese flowering cherry tree, presented as a low-poly hero object.
- Broad class: botanical branching organism; primary domain `object`.
- Confidence: 0.98 that the intended species language is ornamental Sakura; cultivar is not inferable from the image.
- Intended use: persistent real-time browser character with growth, wind, touch, memory, and reveal animation.

## 2. Overall form and silhouette

- Organic, asymmetric branching volume inside an approximately square reference.
- A short, broad trunk occupies the lower third and divides into several leaders through the middle third.
- Crown is wider than the trunk height above the flare and is composed of separated branch gestures rather than one solid dome.
- The reference is close to frontal, but visible overlaps and branch crossings imply shallow front-to-back layering.
- The strongest readable shapes are the rooted trunk, the low lateral boughs, the open central forks, and the broken crown perimeter.

## 3. Macro → meso → micro decomposition

- Macro: ground contact, root flare, aged trunk, two unequal leaders, primary bough system, crown.
- Meso: secondary branches, tertiary flowering twigs, flowering spurs, blossom clusters, bud clusters, sparse leaf clusters.
- Micro: faceted bark planes, horizontal lenticel marks, branch collars, pruning/scar accents, pedicels, five-petal flowers, centers/stamens, notched tips, copper-green leaves, fallen petals.

## 4. Spatial relationships

- Root flare overlaps and embeds into the ground contact rather than ending in visible radial spikes.
- Leaders grow continuously out of the trunk with tapered collars.
- Primary boughs socket into leaders at unequal heights and alternate between front, lateral, and rear depth layers.
- Secondary branches split parent-first, arc toward light, and support finer gravity-responsive twigs.
- Flowering spurs attach to terminal and near-terminal twigs; clustered flowers connect through visible short pedicels.

## 5. Materials and surface

- Bark: dielectric, matte to satin, high roughness, cool taupe/grey-umber albedo with larger polygon facets on the trunk and finer facets on branches.
- Blossoms: dielectric, semi-translucent, soft roughness, warm ivory-to-blush variation; centers are muted ochre/dusty rose.
- Leaves: dielectric, satin-matte, copper/olive/ochre spring palette.
- Ground: diffuse warm paper/soil contact with a broad soft shadow.

## 6. Color and finish

- Background: warm off-white with a restrained vertical light gradient.
- Wood: low-saturation grey-brown with cool shadow planes and restrained warm highlights.
- Flowers: predominantly pale ivory/blush, a smaller warm-pink group, and deep pink limited to buds and centers.
- The visible low-poly finish comes from controlled planar faceting, not uniformly low radial resolution.

## 7. Identity-defining features

- Trunk leans before splitting into unequal leaders.
- One low sweeping bough establishes an identifiable silhouette.
- At least three large negative-space windows remain visible at peak bloom.
- Branch architecture remains readable through the blossom layer.
- Crown has an uneven perimeter, a displaced visual centroid, and several slightly descending terminal gestures.
- Blossoms read as clustered five-petal flowers rather than circular pink filler.

## 8. Uncertainty and single-image limits

- Rear branch topology and full crown depth are hidden; v2 must invent a restrained rear layer and verify it from side/rear captures.
- The reference cannot define physically exact branch attachment or wind behavior.
- Flower translucency and bark roughness are inferred from a stylized render rather than measured material evidence.
- The reference is an emotional and stylistic target, not a requirement for literal pixel tracing.

## Product-specific corrections to the reference

- Increase asymmetry and front-to-back depth beyond the static concept.
- Preserve 25–35% crown negative space even at peak bloom.
- Build major branches as an animation hierarchy; do not merge the entire tree into one rigid mesh.
- Treat every journal as an unbounded nurture event. Rendered detail may be budgeted, but no visible maturity cap may stop development.
